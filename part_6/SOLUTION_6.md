# How You Used AI

I always use AI, even when I don't strictly need to. It works as a true partner and a second set of eyes to give me feedback and help me understand context quickly.

When it comes to the coding sections, I asked the AI to explain concepts I wasn't familiar with like the ISBN-13 digit structure in Part 1, which I hadn't encountered before or to help me diagnose issues in the background job for Part 2. Once I had a clear picture of the problem, I had the AI generate the code for me.

For the tasks that only required text answers (Parts 4, 5, and 6), I wrote everything myself and then used the AI simply to polish the grammar and wording, making sure it never replaced the original ideas I had written down manually.

## What You Verified Yourself

I always review the generated code. Since the coding challenge didn't have a CI pipeline to run tests automatically, I verified them manually. For the update_column method used in part 2, I was already familiar with skipping validations, but I double-checked the Rails documentation just to be sure. As for the grammar and wording polished by the AI, I carefully re-read the response to make sure it hit the mark.

## Hardest Part of the Challenge

Part 2 was the hardest part of this challenge. I was able to spot a few issues on my own just by looking through the code, but due to limited time, I turned to AI to help me catch the rest. It ended up finding several more problems that I hadn't noticed myself.
